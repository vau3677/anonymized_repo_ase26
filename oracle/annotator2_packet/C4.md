C4 requires the inconsistent tuple to reach a decision point that consumes >=1 constituent under an assumption about the whole relation. That establishes semantic sink relevance. Exploitability then asks whether a realistic actor can reach or use the broken tuple to obtain an unauthorized capability or force a concrete effect. We can distinguish:
a: the broken tuple reaches a security/economic/liveness/governance/accounting decision boundary.
b: consuming the broken tuple grants unauthorized ability, denies legitimate ability, corrupts accounting, distorts governance/security decisions, or breaks liveness.
c: feasibility/exploitability evidence: a realistic actor can reach and use the inconsistent state under the protocol’s threat model.
